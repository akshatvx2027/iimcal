Why this exists

The information was never missing. It was just scattered.

A timetable in one PDF, a room change in a WhatsApp forward, the reading for Thursday buried in a course outline nobody opened since Week 1. AcadHub puts all of it in one place and keeps it current, so the answer to "what's tomorrow and what do I need to have read" takes five seconds.

Step one

Put it on your home screen

It's a web app — nothing to download from an app store. Adding it to your home screen makes it open like any other app and is what allows it to send you reminders. Two minutes, once.

  1. Open the link in Safari
    It has to be Safari on iPhone — Chrome cannot install web apps on iOS.
  2. Tap Share ⬆︎ at the bottom
    The square with the arrow coming out of it.
  3. Scroll down → Add to Home Screen
    Then tap Add. The AcadHub icon appears with your other apps.
  4. Open it from the home screen icon
    Not from Safari — from the icon. This matters for the next step.
  5. Profile → turn on Reminders
    Say yes when iOS asks. That's it.
⚠️
iPhones are fussy about this one thing: notifications only work if you opened the app from the home-screen icon. If the reminders switch looks greyed out, you're still in Safari. Close it, tap the icon, try again. Needs iOS 16.4 or newer.
  1. Open the link in Chrome
  2. Tap ⋮ → Add to Home screen
    Chrome often offers an Install app banner on its own — either works.
  3. Confirm Install
    The icon lands in your app drawer like anything from Play Store.
  4. Profile → turn on Reminders
    Allow notifications when Android asks.
  1. Open the link in Chrome or Edge
  2. Click the ⊕ Install icon in the address bar
    Right-hand end, next to the bookmark star.
  3. Profile → Reminders
    Useful if your laptop is the thing that's always open.
💡
You can install it on both. Reminders are per-device, so turn them on wherever you'll actually see them.

What's inside

Four tabs, and that's the whole app

Nothing to learn. Here's what each one is for.

📣

Announcements

Everything the batch needs to know, sorted and tagged — academics, workshops, speaker sessions, general. Time-critical things are pinned to the top and quietly drop off once they're over, so the feed doesn't turn into an archive you scroll past.

🗓️

Calendar

Your actual timetable — session times, rooms, faculty, and the campus events everyone is expected at. Under My courses you pick the electives you've taken, and the calendar becomes yours instead of the batch's.

📚

Subjects

One page per course: faculty, textbook, the grading breakdown, and the session-by-session plan lifted straight from the course outline — which chapter, which case, which assignment is due. Plus any slides or files shared for it.

👤

Profile

Reminders on or off, and that's about it. No settings maze.

Optional: Calendar integration

Sync your timetable with Google Calendar

You can add your AcadHub courses to Google Calendar and link your personal calendar to AcadHub. There's a complete manual with step-by-step instructions, screenshots, and answers to common questions.

Two methods to sync your schedule:

Method 1: Get a personal link to all your courses and add it to Google Calendar. Your schedule updates automatically when classes move or are cancelled.

Method 2: Link your personal Google Calendar to AcadHub using a secret code. Then AcadHub can warn you about schedule conflicts with interviews, projects, or other commitments.

Before you ask

Fair questions

Is this official?

It's built and run by your Academic Representative for the batch — it isn't an institute system. Treat it as the fastest place to find something, and the notice board or PGP office as the last word if the two ever disagree.

What can it see about me?

Your name and IIMC email, from the Google sign-in. That's the lot. It cannot read your mail, your files or your contacts, and it never asks to.

If you choose to connect your own calendar, those entries stay private to you — nobody else in the batch can see them, and neither can I.

Can anyone outside the batch get in?

No. Sign-in is restricted to @email.iimcal.ac.in accounts, and the database checks who you are on every single request rather than trusting the app — so there's no URL you could send someone that would show them anything.

The timetable changed. Will the app know?

Yes — reschedules, room changes and additions are updated centrally and appear for everyone at once, which is rather the point. If you spot something wrong, tell me and it's a two-minute fix for the whole batch.

Too many notifications?

There's one switch in Profile and it turns everything off immediately. The app still works fine without them.

Does it work offline?

Partly — once installed it opens and shows what it last loaded, which covers you in the basement of the library. It needs a connection to pick up anything new.
